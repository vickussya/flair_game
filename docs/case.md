What restrains the wolf? (willpower / pills / ritual?) - willpower (sometimes pills too)

The Client (in the book, a beautiful woman / witch who was robbed)
Name / species: Barbara (witch)
What they want: to find the robber and get back the expensive medicine to cure her client | What they're hiding: the boy upstairs. She is secretly curing the son of Mitropolit Kalligari - head of the Church of the Suffering, the sect that maimed her - with the Black Wind. He dies at midnight without a dose. That is why she wants the thief dead rather than arrested: she believes the medicine was swallowed, and it has to be cut back out of a body.
Scent signature: fake eye, unlimited power, lust
The Informant
Name / role: Maddie (Bunk's daughter) | How they contact Bunk: through the orginiser bracelet
The Culprit / final suspect
Name / species: The Penitent - a mutant zealot of the Church of the Suffering, standing among the four customers as "the widow Lenz" behind a mask of living flesh.
Motive: doctrine. The Communion learned Barbara was cheating Kalligari's son of his holy suffering with a heretic drug. Pain is salvation; a soul healed by witchcraft is a soul robbed of grace. Kalligari would sooner watch his own son die in blessed agony than be saved by the woman he maimed. She stole the Black Wind not to sell it but to make sure the boy never gets his midnight dose - and to ruin Barbara with it.
Method: she swallowed it on the spot. She is not human, so the dose that kills a man did not kill her. The marked vial never crossed the threshold because it was never carried out - she left it behind empty, back among the shelves. The energy seal tracks the glass, not medicine dissolved in a living body. Her flesh-mask smothered the Black Wind bleeding off her skin, so Bunk never smelled the theft walk past him. The recording jammer was hers.
Mistake that exposes them: the mask is too good. She smells exactly, precisely like a frail old human - and nothing alive smells that clean. A flawless scent is a manufactured one.
Scent signature (their "tell"): incense and candle-tallow, evenly distributed, with no skin, no breath and no age underneath it. She also smokes the same expensive tobacco as Rook, a cult habit - which is why Bunk reads her trail in the alley as Rook's.
Other NPCs / witnesses (list 2–4):

The jittery merchant - came to settle a debt of medicine. Sweat and cheap liquor.

PART 5 — The Case (design this BEFORE any tech)
A mystery must be solvable backwards first. Design the truth, then hide it.
LEVEL 1 - "THE HOLLOW VIAL"
Same world, same spine as the book's "A Vial of Wind", a different answer. Everything a reader
knows stays true; only the solution changes. The level is built so that a reader who solved the
book walks confidently into the wrong answer - twice.

5.1 — The truth (players never see this directly):
What actually happened, start to finish: The Communion of the Church of the Suffering learned that
Barbara was curing Mitropolit Kalligari's son with the Black Wind. By their doctrine a healing
bought from a witch robs a soul of its grace, so Kalligari would rather his son die in blessed
agony. The Communion sent the Penitent - a mutant zealot in a mask of living flesh - into the
droggery as a harmless old widow. She waited for Barbara to go upstairs, triggered a jammer that
wiped the security recording, opened the vial of Black Wind and swallowed it where she stood. She
put the empty vial back among the shelves and walked out. The energy seal never sang because the
marked glass never left the room, and Bunk never smelled the theft go past him because the
flesh-mask smothered it. Barbara saw the gap where a full vial should be and called it a theft.
Who did it, how, why: The Penitent. She swallowed the dose - lethal to a human, survivable by her -
and carried it out inside her own body. She did it on Kalligari's will, to let the boy die on
schedule and to ruin Barbara with it.
Timeline of the crime: Midday, the droggery holds four customers. Barbara steps upstairs. The
jammer wipes the recording. The Penitent drinks, reshelves the hollow vial and leaves. Barbara
returns, finds the gap, and hires Bunk at his 2,000 rate with 10,000 offered if the thief is
brought before midnight. The boy upstairs dies at midnight without a dose.

5.2 — The logline the player starts with:
"Barbara the witch hires Bunk to find who stole a vial of Black Wind from her droggery, and to do
it before midnight, and she will not say why the hour matters."

5.3 — Clue chain (each clue should point to the next; smell-clues marked 👃):
1. 🔍 THE HOLLOW VIAL - the droggery, back among the shelves. The marked vial, present and empty.
   Leads to: the medicine left the room without the glass, which is why the seal never fired.
2. 👃 THE FOUR LINGERING SCENTS - the droggery air, matched against the damaged hologram. Bunk
   cannot smell a recording; he smells what the four customers left in the room. Three read as
   ordinary people. The fourth is logged now and not understood until clue 5.
   Leads to: Borlow's scent is the strongest thread, so Bunk follows it.
3. 👃 DEATH AND EXPENSIVE TOBACCO - the service alley, where Borlow's trail simply stops. The Ant
   points at the leaf: only one kind of man in the Privy smokes it.
   Leads to: Rook, and through him the Doctor's house and the Church.
4. 🔍 THE SILVER CRUCIFIX AND THE DOCTRINE - smelled under Rook's collar at the Doctor's, then read
   in carved stone across the street. PAIN IS SALVATION. A healing bought cheaply from a witch is a
   soul cheated of its suffering.
   Leads to: the motive. Not to Rook - to the Church behind him.
5. 👃 THE TOO-PERFECT SCENT - unlocked when Maddie's call clears Borlow and the first solution
   collapses. Re-reading clue 2 on the mind board (6.10): the widow's incense and candle-tallow has
   no skin, no breath and no age underneath. Nothing alive smells that clean.
   Leads to: a mask of living flesh - and something wearing one is not human, and something not
   human could swallow the dose and live.
Required to unlock the finale: 3 clues - 1, 2 and 5. Clues 3 and 4 supply the motive and the Rook
decoy; the accusation stands without them, but the case does not make sense without them.

5.4 — Red herrings / "filler" (your concept's пълнеж/заблуда — dead-end scents):
BORLOW - the strongest scent in the room, an old human with a cough who left early. Everything
points at him, and the book's own answer is that he did it with a jammer and an aquarium. He is
innocent: Maddie reaches him alive across the district at midday.
THE AQUARIUM - Black Wind dissolves in water and the seaweed tanks are right there. It is the
solution to the book's case and it is wrong here. The water is only water.
ROOK - expensive tobacco, a silver crucifix, a cult member inside the Doctor's crew. In the book
this is the thief. Here he only prays there. The tobacco in the alley is the Penitent's; they share
the habit because they share the church.
"IT CANNOT HAVE BEEN SWALLOWED" - Barbara says the dose kills a human on the tongue. She is right,
and that is precisely why nobody looks for someone who swallowed it.

5.5 — The solution moment — what makes the player sure who did it?
Three things sitting on the board together. The vial was left behind empty, so the medicine left
the room inside something. One of the four scents has nothing living underneath it. And the dose
kills a human, so whatever carried it out was not one. The widow Lenz is the only customer who was
never a person - and she is the only one Bunk dismissed in the first five minutes.

5.6 — Endings and the finale
The level is won by naming the culprit. The fight decides what winning costs.
- Correct accusation, fight won: the Black Wind is cut out of the Penitent in time, the boy lives,
  Barbara pays the full 10,000, maximum units carried into the next level.
- Correct accusation, fight lost: the case is solved and the level completes, but midnight passes
  and the boy dies. Barbara pays the base 2,000 - she got her answer, not her medicine. Reduced
  units. This is 6.8's "the case is solved but there is no justice for the client".
- Wrong accusation: not an ending. The board is checked as a whole (6.10) and rejected. It costs
  time and Barbara's patience and lowers the fee, and the player goes back to the case.
- Midnight reached with no correct accusation: the boy dies, Barbara pays nothing, minimum units.
Bunk fights the Penitent in human form. The werewolf transformation is deliberately NOT in this
level - see 6.7; it is developed in level 2.
The fight plays as a 2D vision, not as 3D combat - see 6.8. It is still a fight, it still has a
winner, and it is still the level's climax; it is drawn rather than simulated.
Bunk never dies. Losing the fight costs units, not his life - the units carry into later levels, so
a loss here is felt in the bigger project rather than as a game over.
The fight can be retried, but the clock keeps running while you retry, so enough failed attempts
cost the boy anyway.
Midnight advances per player action, not in real time. A real clock punishes thinking, and thinking
is the thing this game wants the player doing. Investigating, travelling and accusing spend time;
standing still and reading the board does not.
